# Worked Example — Seaside Hiroshima Tram Scene

> **Case-study scope:** This is one application of the workflow, not a restriction on the Skill. The same geometry-first method can be adapted to cars, trains, railway layouts, miniature streets, architectural models, ports, industrial scenes, roads, and other physical model photography.

## Original strengths to lock

- vintage tram geometry and three-quarter front view
- two rails and rail grooves through the crossing
- crossing barriers and signals
- road markings
- autumn tree as natural framing
- shallow depth of field
- original camera position and composition

## World setting

- location: quiet Japanese seaside tram crossing
- season: late autumn
- time: blue hour, roughly 20–30 minutes after sunset
- air: slightly humid coastal haze
- distant evidence: faint sea, low buildings/sheds, utility poles, sparse wires, tiny harbor lights

## Lighting hierarchy

1. existing station lamps — dominant warm atmosphere
2. vintage tram headlight — warm ivory incandescent
3. crossing red lamp — small operational accent
4. rooftop route number 9 — dim period backlit lightbox
5. tram interior — low, continuous, irregular warm-neutral illumination
6. harbor lights — tiny distant bokeh

## Physical headlight logic

The tram faces toward the lower-left foreground.
Headlight influence follows the tram axis.
Existing rail tops catch the first thin reflections.
Track bed receives only a small exposure lift.
Road markings receive only faint secondary reflection.
No circular spotlight and no erased track detail.

## Lessons from failure

- generic “photorealistic” prompts can redraw the tram;
- “illuminate the ground” can create a fake light patch;
- repeated edits can erase rails and distort text;
- bright uniform windows look like a display model;
- once the concept works, return to the original and run one consolidated master prompt.
